The MIT Department of Mechanical Engineering featured two studies that PAIRS Lab Director **Professor Fangqiang Ding** contributed to during his postdoctoral research at MIT’s 77 Lab. Published in *IEEE Transactions on Neural Systems and Rehabilitation Engineering* (TNSRE), the studies explore how video recorded with devices such as smartphones can support motor assessment in people with rare neurological disorders.

Collecting enough labeled video to train a model is difficult when a disorder affects few people. Both studies addressed this challenge through staged transfer learning. They started with models pretrained on large human movement datasets, adapted them using data from another condition that affects movement, and then fine-tuned them on the rare disorder under study.

- **Gross motor function in children with metachromatic leukodystrophy (MLD).** Ding is the first author of a study that estimates gross motor function from de-identified body poses extracted from clinical videos. Data from children with cerebral palsy provided an intermediate training step before the model was fine-tuned on the MLD cohort. [AI-Based Digital Assessment of Gross Motor Function in Metachromatic Leukodystrophy](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/11657452/).
- **Balance in people with NF2-related schwannomatosis (NF2-SWN).** Ding co-authored a study that uses smartphone video to estimate balance. The team adapted its model using data from people with Parkinson’s disease before fine-tuning it on the NF2-SWN cohort. [Bridge-Disease Curriculum Transfer Enables Smartphone-Based Balance Assessment in Rare Neurologic Disorders](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/11686857/).
